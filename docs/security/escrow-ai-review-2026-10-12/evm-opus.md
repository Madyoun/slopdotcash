# Reviewer: opus / EVM

Scope: `contracts/evm/src/ProjectEscrow.sol` (ProjectEscrow, EscrowFactory) and `contracts/evm/script/DeployBase.s.sol`, `DeployBaseSepolia.s.sol` at bbcc87b (scratch copy byte-identical, checked with diff). PoC and invariant tests: `scratchpad/review/evm/test/OpusBase.sol`, `OpusFindings.t.sol`, `OpusInvariant.t.sol`, `OpusTokens.t.sol`. All 19 tests PASS (each PoC passes because it demonstrates the behavior). Invariant suite run with `FOUNDRY_INVARIANT_RUNS=512 FOUNDRY_INVARIANT_DEPTH=60` (30,720 calls per invariant, about 1,280 successful pays); the harness was checked against a deliberately false invariant (it fails as expected).

No Critical or High finding. Nothing lets an unprivileged party move money, choose amount or destination, double pay, or take reserved funds. The findings below are about the binding-delay security model: what it actually protects, and what happens when a trusted role fails.

## Findings

### F-1: A binding that matures while its actor has no award becomes a pre-armed redirect with no delay and no veto at payment time
- Severity: Medium  Confidence: high (mechanics), medium (severity)
- Location: contracts/evm/src/ProjectEscrow.sol:159-175, 178-185 (cancel only while `block.timestamp < activatesAt`), 187-191 (pay checks only the binding's own `activatesAt`); README.md:26-33; contract NatSpec :12-14 ("a stolen authority key cannot pay itself before the sponsor can react")
- Description: The delay and the veto are measured from when the binding is made, not from when money becomes payable to it. Bindings persist forever and any later award to that actor pays the active binding at once. A binding made for an actor with no award in the vault puts no money at risk while it is pending, so the owner has nothing to react to. After `bindingDelay` it is final (`cancelBinding` reverts) and silently covers every future award to that actor. In the intended flow (docs/payouts-mvp.md:15) contributors register wallets before they have awards, so a malicious binding for a likely future contributor looks the same as a normal early registration. The owner cannot check a `claimDigest` against anything on chain.
- Scenario: (1) The compromised `identityAuthority` calls `bindDestination(alice, attacker, 0, d, exp)` for one or more active repo contributors who have no award in this vault yet. (2) 48h pass. The owner sees nothing urgent: no award is reserved for those actors. (3) Weeks later the owner calls `commitAward(id, alice, 1000e6, src)` for the monthly cycle. (4) In the same block, any relayer calls `pay(id, 1)` and the attacker gets 980 USDC. The owner never had a delay window tied to that money. Rotating an active binding of an actor whose awards are all paid works the same way.
- Evidence: `OpusFindingsTest.test_F1_preArmedBindingPaysInstantlyAfterCommit`: PASS (shows owner cancel reverting after activation, and payment to the attacker in the commit block).
- Recommendation: Tie the review window to the money. For example, record `lastCommittedAt[actorId]` in `commitAward`. Make `pay` require `block.timestamp >= max(binding.activatesAt, lastCommittedAt[actor] + bindingDelay)`. Let `cancelBinding` succeed while `block.timestamp` is below that same maximum. Then every award gets a fresh owner review of its current destination. At minimum, the README and owner tooling should state that the owner must audit and cancel every unverifiable binding within 48h, including for actors with no award, because an active binding can never be cancelled.

### F-2: The owner can block a committed award indefinitely by cancelling every binding; the award is then locked for good
- Severity: Medium  Confidence: high
- Location: contracts/evm/src/ProjectEscrow.sol:178-185; README.md:29-33 ("The owner can therefore delay a contributor's payout")
- Description: `cancelBinding` has no limit on count or rate and needs no reason. Every new authority binding gets a fresh delay, so the owner can cancel each one in turn, forever. There is no award cancellation or expiry and nothing can sweep the award, so the reserved gross stays locked in the vault for good. The owner cannot recover it either, which makes this pure griefing or coercion rather than theft. The brief's policy says the owner must not be able to "indefinitely block a committed award beyond the documented delay"; the README calls this power a "delay", but on chain it has no bound.
- Scenario: (1) The owner commits Alice's award. (2) The authority binds Alice's verified wallet. (3) The owner calls `cancelBinding(alice, v)` within 48h. (4) Steps 2 and 3 repeat for every rebind. Alice is never paid; `reservedPrincipal` stays at 980 USDC; `withdrawUnused` cannot reach it.
- Evidence: `OpusFindingsTest.test_F2_ownerCanBlockCommittedAwardIndefinitely`: PASS (25 bind/cancel rounds; pay reverts each time; reserve intact; owner cannot withdraw it).
- Recommendation: This pulls against F-1 and F-3, where the owner's veto is the only defence. Options: limit vetoes per actor (for example, after N cancellations the next binding gets a longer delay with no veto), or add an independent guardian or contributor-side override. If the unbounded power is accepted, say "indefinitely" in the README and show a "held by owner veto" state per PAY-01, rather than "delay".

### F-3: The immutable identity authority leaves no recovery path; one key compromise turns every unpaid award into a permanent lock or eventual theft
- Severity: Medium  Confidence: high
- Location: contracts/evm/src/ProjectEscrow.sol:38 (`identityAuthority` immutable), :166-173 (an authority rotation overwrites an ACTIVE binding and resets its delay), :178-185; EscrowFactory :265 (immutable for every vault the factory creates)
- Description: The README presents the delay plus owner cancel as the mitigation for a stolen authority key (README.md:26-33). The mitigation never ends. The authority can never be replaced, and the same key that the operator uses to rebind honestly stays usable by the attacker. The attacker can rotate an active honest binding, which destroys it (the owner can cancel the rogue version but cannot restore the old one). Any honest rebind can be overwritten by the attacker within the same window. The owner must win every 48h race forever; missing one window means theft. If the key is simply lost, no actor can ever be bound again, so every award to an actor without an active binding is locked for good.
- Scenario: (1) Alice has an active honest binding v1 and a reserved award. (2) The attacker holding the authority key calls `bindDestination(alice, attacker, 1, ...)`, giving v2 pending; the relayer's `pay(id, 1)` now reverts. (3) The owner cancels v2. Alice now has no payable binding. (4) The operator rebinds Alice (v3); the attacker rotates again (v4). (5) If the owner misses one 48h window, `pay(id, 4)` sends 980 USDC to the attacker.
- Evidence: `OpusFindingsTest.test_F3_compromisedAuthorityConvertsToLockOrTheft`: PASS.
- Recommendation: Add a timelocked authority replacement. It could be controlled by the owner together with an independent party, with a delay at least `bindingDelay`. Alternatively, add an owner emergency switch that makes bindings created after a timestamp unpayable until a new authority is installed. If immutability is kept on purpose, document the incident playbook: on compromise, every reserved award is stuck unless the owner cancels forever, and new awards must go to a new vault.

### F-4: `bindingDelay` has no on-chain bounds; the README's `MIN_PRODUCTION_BINDING_DELAY` does not exist anywhere in the repo
- Severity: Low  Confidence: high
- Location: contracts/evm/src/ProjectEscrow.sol:102, 116, 172, 180; EscrowFactory :272-282; README.md:33-35; script/DeployBase.s.sol:19, script/DeployBaseSepolia.s.sol:16
- Description: `grep -rn MIN_PRODUCTION_BINDING_DELAY` finds only the README. Neither the factory nor the vault checks the delay. With `bindingDelay == 0`, `activatesAt == block.timestamp`, so `cancelBinding` always reverts and the owner veto never applies. With a delay near `type(uint64).max`, `uint64(block.timestamp) + bindingDelay` overflows and every `bindDestination` panics, so every award is locked for good. Both deploy scripts hard-code 48 hours and the adapter checks the deployed value against the manifest (adapter.ts:147), so this is reachable only by a non-script deployment or a bad manifest.
- Scenario: A factory deployed with delay 0 (for example, a copy of the integration config) lets a stolen authority key bind and pay in one block.
- Evidence: `test_F4_zeroDelayMakesCancelUnreachable`: PASS; `test_F4_hugeDelayBricksBinding`: PASS.
- Recommendation: In the `EscrowFactory` and `ProjectEscrow` constructors, require `MIN <= bindingDelay <= MAX` (for example, 48h to 30d), or define the constant the README names. Keep test deployments on a separate code path or parameter.

### F-5: `cancelBinding` repeats on an already-cancelled binding and emits duplicate `BindingCancelled` events at any later time
- Severity: Low  Confidence: high
- Location: contracts/evm/src/ProjectEscrow.sol:180-184
- Description: After a cancel, `activatesAt == type(uint64).max`, so `block.timestamp >= activatesAt` stays false forever. A second, third or later `cancelBinding(actor, v)` succeeds and emits another `BindingCancelled`, even a year later. State does not change, but an indexer that counts vetoes, or treats each event as a new owner action, will over-report. Solana's `veto_binding` uses `init` and cannot repeat, so the two chains differ here.
- Evidence: `test_F5_cancelIsRepeatable`: PASS (3 events for one binding, the last 365 days later).
- Recommendation: Revert when `binding.activatesAt == type(uint64).max`.

### F-6 (Informational): The identity authority is a routing key, and the PRD forbids a Slop-held one; the contract does not enforce the PAY-05 wallet proof
- Severity: Informational  Confidence: high
- Location: contracts/evm/src/ProjectEscrow.sol:159-175; docs/slop-product-requirements.md:337 (PAY-05), :626, :813; docs/payouts-mvp.md:17
- Description: `bindDestination` takes any destination from `identityAuthority` alone; no destination-wallet signature or other proof is checked on chain. PAY-05 says "An attacker who signs a victim's actor ID with the attacker's wallet must not bind or receive the victim's award" and keeps binding blocked until the proof design is approved. PRD :813 says "No Slop-held key originates, routes or redirects a payment", and :626 says no component may stand in a Slop signature for that proof. The brief describes the authority as a Slop-run service key. If that is how it is deployed, the contract makes a Slop key the sole router of every payout, which conflicts with the PRD rows above (docs/payouts-mvp.md:17 calls it a "disclosed routing trust boundary"). Who holds the key is an operations question; nothing on chain prevents it from being Slop's.
- Recommendation: Settle who holds `identityAuthority` before any deployment, record it in the manifest, and cite PAY-05 in the README so a reader does not treat the merged code as approval of the binding design.

### F-7 (Informational): Smaller robustness notes
- Severity: Informational  Confidence: high
- Location / notes:
  - :204-215: a withdrawal can carry a 100% fee. For example, gross 1 after cumulative 9 gives fee 1 and net 0; `sendExact` skips the zero transfer and `UnusedWithdrawn(net=0)` is emitted. This follows from the cumulative rule, never exceeds gross, and is harmless (test `test_withdrawFeeCanEqualGross`: PASS).
  - :36, :107, :214: `refundDestination` must equal `owner`, so the parameter carries no information. If Circle blacklists the owner, `withdrawUnused` and `deposit` revert forever. Free funds can then leave only through `commitAward` or `chargeService`, with no alternate refund route.
  - :284-294: the registrar can permanently use up a project ID in a factory by creating it with a wrong owner; recovery needs a new factory. This fits the disclosed registrar trust boundary, but it is not stated.
  - A vault deployed directly, or by a second factory with the same constructor arguments (same projectId, token, owner, feeRecipient, authority, delay), has byte-identical runtime code, because the immutables are equal. So `codeSha256` plus the getter checks (adapter.ts:105-150) cannot prove a vault came from the reviewed factory; only the configured vault address does that. Off-chain registration should also check `factory.vaults(projectId) == vault`.
  - :51, :145: `consumedSources` is redundant, because `id == awardId(sourceDigest)` is a keccak bijection within one vault. It is harmless.
  - The scripts emit `FactoryDeployed`/`TestFactoryDeployed` from the script contract, so these are local simulation logs, not on-chain evidence. Neither script nor the factory checks `identityAuthority != feeRecipient` or `identityAuthority != registrar` (msg.sender). The Base USDC (0x8335...2913), Base Sepolia USDC (0x036C...CF7e) and fee recipient (0x8f77...9b86, matching PRD :814) constants are correct.

## Checked and found sound
- Conservation identities. A fuzzed handler (`OpusInvariantTest`) runs random deposit, commit, bind, cancel, warp, pay (random caller), stale-version pay, withdraw, chargeService, claimFees and direct donation. Across 512 runs x 60 depth it holds: `totalDeposited == free + reservedPrincipal + reservedFees + totalPaid + totalPayoutFees + totalGrossWithdrawn + totalServiceFees`; `vaultBalance == free + reservedPrincipal + reservedFees + accruedFees + donations` (direct transfers only add an unaccounted surplus, as documented); `accruedFees + claimed == totalPayoutFees + totalWithdrawalFees + totalServiceFees`, with the fee recipient's balance equal to claimed; `totalWithdrawalFees == totalGrossWithdrawn / 10`; per-award `fee == gross/50`; `reservedPrincipal` and `reservedFees` equal the sums over unpaid awards; no award paid twice; every payment goes to the binding current at that instant, never before activation; a non-current version never pays. Hand derivation agrees for each function (:130-138, :141-154, :187-201, :204-216, :220-229, :232-238).
- Overflow: every counter is bounded by `totalDeposited` (checked uint64, :136), except `totalWithdrawalFees <= totalGrossWithdrawn`, so nothing else can overflow first. `uint64(block.timestamp)` (:172) is safe until year 2^64 s. `expectedVersion + 1` (:171) needs 2^64 bindings. `award.gross - award.fee` (:192) and the withdrawal `gross - fee` (:214) cannot underflow (`fee <= gross` in both).
- Fee splitting: withdrawal fee is the increase in floor(cumulative/10), so splitting or ordering cannot lower it. Payout-fee rounding (gross/50) lets an owner save less than 1 micro-unit per award by splitting, far below gas; this is the documented tiny-award rounding.
- The owner cannot touch reserves: `withdrawUnused` and `chargeService` are bounded by `freeBalance` (:206, :223), and no path moves reserved principal except `pay`.
- Permissionless `pay`: the caller picks neither amount nor destination; `expectedBindingVersion` must equal the current version (:191), so a relayer cannot pick a stale binding, and a rotation in flight makes the old payment revert. `award.paid` is set before the external call (:193).
- Cancel and pay windows do not overlap. Cancel requires `now < activatesAt`; pay requires `now >= activatesAt` (:180, :191). At exactly `activatesAt`, cancel reverts and pay succeeds. A cancelled binding (`activatesAt = uint64.max`) never pays. A version that does not exist yet cannot be pre-cancelled, because `expectedVersion` must equal the current one. A rotation after a cancel waits its own full delay (`test_sound_cancelledNeverPaysAndRotationWaits`: PASS). A stolen authority key cannot outrun the veto by rotating faster, since every rotation resets the clock (:172). A 48h delay is far longer than Base's roughly 12h forced-inclusion window, so a censoring sequencer cannot by itself run out the owner's cancel window.
- Award ID collisions: `keccak256(abi.encode(chainid, vault, sourceDigest))` (:121-123) binds chain and vault. Another vault cannot commit this vault's ID (`test_sound_awardIdIsVaultBound`: PASS). The factory refuses a duplicate projectId (:286). CREATE2 cannot be redeployed because ProjectEscrow has no selfdestruct.
- Token handling (`OpusTokensTest`, all PASS): a token that returns no data works; one that returns `false` or 64 bytes reverts with `TransferFailed`; a fee-on-transfer token reverts cleanly on `deposit` (exact-delta check :134), with no drift. A reentrant token callback into `claimFees` during `pay` is blocked by `nonReentrant` and the whole pay rolls back. Both sides of `sendExact` (:240-249) are checked exactly; `destination == address(this)` is rejected at bind (:168), and `feeRecipient == this` in the constructor (:108).
- Authority limits: the authority cannot change amounts, free funds, or paid awards (`bindDestination` writes only `bindings`). Owner and authority must differ (:108). The authority transaction has no replayable blob.
- Reentrancy on the functions without the modifier (`commitAward`, `bindDestination`, `cancelBinding`): none of them makes an external call, and each is role-gated (owner or authority).

## Divergences between EVM and Solana
- Binding scope: EVM bindings live per vault (:52), so each vault gets its own delay and veto. Solana's `WalletBinding` PDA is `[wallet, authority, network, actor]` (lib.rs:384), shared by every project with that authority, and the veto is per project (lib.rs:397). On Solana, F-1 is stronger: a binding made via project A's `bind_wallet` matures for project B, and `WalletBound` carries no project field (lib.rs:521-527), so B's owner may never connect it to their project.
- Repeat cancel: on EVM `cancelBinding` can be repeated (F-5); Solana `veto_binding` uses `init` and cannot.
- Fees: EVM fees accrue and are pulled by `claimFees`, so a blocked fee recipient blocks only that call. Solana sends fees inside `pay`, `withdraw` and `charge_service` to a `fee_account` that only has to be owned by `fee_recipient` (lib.rs:414), so a frozen fee account blocks payouts there.
- Bind expiry: EVM `bindDestination` has `expiresAt` (:164, :169); Solana `bind_wallet` has none and relies on blockhash expiry.
- Award ID: EVM includes `block.chainid`. Solana's `award_id_for` is sha256("slop-escrow-award", project, source) (lib.rs:290-292), with no cluster domain, so the same program ID, owner and project_id on devnet and mainnet give the same project PDA and the same award IDs. An off-chain ledger keyed by award ID must also key by cluster.
- Creation: an EVM registrar gates vault creation (:285); Solana `initialize` is open to any owner key (lib.rs:340).
- Free-balance checks: Solana also checks `vault.amount >= reserved + x` on commit, withdraw and charge (lib.rs:71, 211, 262); EVM relies on accounting plus the exact-delta `sendExact`. Both are sound.
- Delay type: EVM `uint64` with no floor or cap (F-4); Solana `i64 >= 0` with no floor; `checked_add` overflow there makes `activates_at` error, which bricks pay and veto for that binding, the same failure as EVM's bind-time panic.

## Open questions for the lead
- Is the owner's unbounded veto (F-2) meant to be "indefinite"? If the policy says "delay" means bounded, F-2 should be High (owner exceeds documented power, no money moved).
- Who holds `identityAuthority` in production (F-6)? If it is Slop, it conflicts with PRD :813 and with CLAUDE.md's "never routes" principle.
- The adapter's `verifyBinding` (adapter.ts:323-373) returns `activatesAt` but does not show cancellation or supersession. I did not review the adapter, but its callers must not treat a `DestinationBound` event as payable without reading current `bindings(actor)` state.
- F-1's fix lengthens every payout by `bindingDelay` after commit. Product should confirm that fits the 14-day review and the dispatch SLA.
